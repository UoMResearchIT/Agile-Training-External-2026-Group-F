# Italian Cuisine

Italian cooking is one of the world's most beloved culinary traditions — not because it is elaborate, but because it is honest. At its heart, Italian food is built on a simple principle: let good ingredients speak for themselves.

Rooted in centuries of regional tradition, Italian cuisine varies enormously from north to south. The northern regions favour rich risottos, butter-based sauces and egg pasta, while the south leans on olive oil, tomatoes, and dried pasta shapes. Yet across all these differences, a common philosophy holds: simplicity, seasonality, and respect for the ingredient.

Many of Italy's most iconic dishes were born from cucina povera — the cooking of the poor — where resourcefulness turned humble pantry staples into something extraordinary. A handful of garlic, olive oil and pasta. A broth stretched with whatever vegetables were to hand. Bread, tomato, and a little cheese. These are not dishes of scarcity; they are dishes of ingenuity, and they have endured for good reason.

The recipes in this folder reflect that spirit. From the quiet confidence of a Margherita pizza — where the quality of the tomato and the freshness of the mozzarella carry everything — to the restorative depth of a minestrone and the bare, punchy elegance of spaghetti aglio e olio, each dish asks only that you pay attention to what you're working with.

Cook these recipes as they were intended: without shortcuts, without substitutions where they matter, and with a little patience.
